While testing Coy on my phone, I really liked how clean and low-friction the flow is—especially picking someone straight from contacts and knowing it stays completely secret unless both people match.

After going through everything (the OTP login, adding a crush, email verification, invites, and the extra slots screen), here are two features I think would genuinely improve the experience:

---

### 1. Letting users recycle an inactive crush slot after 2–3 weeks

Right now, you get a small, fixed number of slots. That makes sense because it forces you to pick people you actually care about instead of spamming everyone.

The problem is what happens right after: if the 2 or 3 people you pick never download Coy, ignore the SMS, or are already seeing someone, your account is pretty much frozen. You can't add anyone new without paying or badgering three friends to sign up and add crushes of their own. After checking the app a couple of times and seeing nothing change, most users will just delete it or forget it exists.

If Coy let users swap out or release a slot after, say, 14 to 21 days of no activity, it would make a huge difference. People wouldn't overthink their choices so much when first signing up, and they wouldn't hit a dead end if their crushes just never join the app.

---

### 2. Putting campus or circle context in the anonymous text message

When you add a crush by phone, Coy sends them a message saying:
*"Someone you know has added you as a crush on the Coy App. Find out if it's mutual at app.getcoy.com"*

In reality, most people get a ton of random spam texts every week. If an unknown number sends an anonymous link saying "someone likes you", a lot of people are just going to assume it's a phishing link or scam and swipe it away.

Since Coy already has college email verification, the text could include broad, verified context—something like:
*"Someone from [Your College] added you as a secret crush on Coy. Find out if it's mutual at app.getcoy.com"*

It immediately stops looking like automated spam and turns into something intriguing. If someone knows a person from their own campus or batch has a crush on them, they're ten times more likely to actually tap the link and sign up to find out who it is. And because a college has thousands of students, the sender stays completely anonymous.
